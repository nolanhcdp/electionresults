Precinct lines changed after 2022, so these races are measured against how a typical Democrat in the same kind of race did in the same precincts in 2024. State House and Senate rows are the Howard County part of each district.

Most of the gain came from voters changing how they voted

Take the Secretary of State race. The Democratic share of the vote in Howard County went from 34.9% in 2022 to 47.6% this year. About 2 points of that gain came from who showed up. About 10 came from voters choosing our candidate, including people who usually vote Republican.

Turnout didn't fail. Our voters showed up.

The total number of ballots was about the same as 2022: 25,383, up 1%. But who cast them changed. Turnout among voters our data scores as Democratic-leaning rose almost 8 points, to 48%. Among Republican-leaning voters it rose 1.5 points, to 39.5%.

Why we put persuasion first

Two reasons. First, the pool. Howard County's voters lean about 77% Republican to 23% Democratic, so there are far more voters to persuade than there are Democrats left to turn out.

Second, the work. Raising turnout by even 5 points takes a field team knocking doors and making calls every day for months. And a persuaded voter counts twice: one more vote for us and one fewer for them.

Persuade 5% of their voters

It worked. In Cathy Stover's district, Republican-leaning voters outnumbered Democratic-leaning voters at the polls, 2,640 to 1,812, and she won with 2,604 votes. Alisha Davenport got 6,314 votes in a township where 4,083 Democratic-leaning voters cast a ballot.

Turnout still matters, and there is room to grow: about 7,300 Democratic-leaning voters did not vote. Most of them rarely vote in midterms, so reaching them takes steady work over years, not a push in October.

Democratic-leaning and Republican-leaning come from voter-file support scores. They are estimates of how groups of voters lean, not a record of how anyone voted.

The biggest swings came where we added mail and digital

A few local races got extra help: direct mail, digital ads, or both. Measured against a typical 2024 race in the same precincts, those races swung an average of 26.6 points. The other local races swung 17.6. Hannah Guillaume, who had a digital ad, got the most votes of the six Center Township Board candidates.

Candidates and neighborhoods differ from race to race too, so read this as a pattern. Swing compares each race with the average margin of 2024's comparable races in the same precincts.

Inside Kokomo it was nearly even

Across the six countywide races, the average margin was R+0.8 inside the city and R+37.0 outside it. We carried two of the six city council districts and lost two more by under five points.
